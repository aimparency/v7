# AI-transformation prospect briefs

Public facts inform questions; they do not establish internal pain. Validate every workflow with its owner before proposing automation.

## Wilde Möhre

### Public context checked 22 July 2026

- The 2026 Wilde Möhre Original runs 21–24 August in Drebkau and is publicly described as the last Wilde Möhre Original.
- The public site describes 12 floors plus workshops, talks, theatre, camping, accessibility processes, local-resident arrangements, ecological infrastructure, multiple participation/application paths, and operational sound monitoring.
- Ticket support is handled by Höme, demonstrating that at least one operational boundary already crosses organizations.
- The operator is Wilde Möhre GmbH.

### Timing decision

Do not suggest a broad transformation in the final month before the event. That would create risk when the organization has the least spare attention. Ask for either:

1. a short owner conversation now, explicitly scheduling substantive work after the festival; or
2. a post-event diagnostic focused on retaining operational knowledge and deciding what should survive the final Original format.

Only touch a pre-event workflow if its owner independently names it, its scope is tiny, rollback is immediate, and failure cannot affect safety, access, payments, artists, crew, residents, or regulatory compliance.

### Discovery hypotheses

Ask which, if any, is real:

- **Operational-memory handover:** Where does knowledge about producing the event currently live, and what will be lost after the final Original?
- **Applications and onboarding:** Are contributor, crew, service-provider, program, or gastronomy applications repeatedly routed and reconciled by hand?
- **Cross-team dependency visibility:** Which late dependencies routinely require an owner to chase several teams?
- **Post-event reconciliation:** Which incident, supplier, staffing, finance, or learning records are hardest to assemble after the event?
- **External-boundary coordination:** Where do handoffs to providers such as ticket support create duplicated communication or missing ownership?

The strongest Aimparency-specific wedge is likely not “automate a festival.” It is “turn the hard-won operating knowledge and decision rights of the final Original into an inspectable, reusable organizational memory.”

### Stop conditions

- No access to attendee, staff, artist, payment, or incident data before written authorization and minimization.
- No autonomous communication to external people during the first diagnostic.
- No safety, access-control, environmental-compliance, or payment decision delegated in a first pilot.
- Pause if the workflow owner cannot provide a baseline or cannot personally stop the pilot.

## Herzog Records

### Public context checked 22 July 2026

- Herzog Records GmbH is an owner-represented Hamburg label founded in 2006.
- Its public description emphasizes a small team, long-term artist support, following contacts, media observation, studio/festival activity, and grant applications.
- Its public catalogue includes several releases in March and April 2026.
- Public activity spans label catalogue, artists, streaming/webshop links, media, stages, and booking-facing surfaces.

### Discovery hypotheses

Ask where the owner currently acts as the human integration layer:

- **Release orchestration:** coordinating masters, metadata, artwork, dates, distribution links, promotion assets, approvals, and follow-ups;
- **Relationship follow-up:** remembering conversations, promises, media opportunities, venues, festivals, and next actions without degrading personal relationships;
- **Catalogue consistency:** keeping release and artist information aligned across owned pages, streaming destinations, shops, and campaign material;
- **Grant/application workflow:** reusing stable label/artist facts while tracking deadlines, eligibility, attachments, approvals, and outcomes;
- **Campaign learning:** retaining what worked across releases without reducing artistic judgment to engagement metrics.

Do not begin with automatic content generation. Herzog's stated value is attentive, personal artist development; automation that produces more generic communication could destroy rather than create value. Begin with private coordination and memory around human relationships.

### Candidate first diagnostic

Map one recent release from decision to post-release follow-up. Record:

- stages and accountable people;
- repeated data and duplicated entry;
- approval and artistic-judgment points;
- missed/late handoffs and their cost;
- systems of record;
- one reversible coordination loop that can run without contacting artists or media autonomously.

The likely first pilot is a private release-control loop that notices missing dependencies, assembles existing facts, and asks the responsible human for decisions. The owner remains the voice and relationship holder.

## Common commercial framing

Sell the diagnostic, not the predicted automation. The deliverable is an owner-approved workflow graph, baseline, decision-right map, ranked opportunities, and a go/no-go pilot recommendation. A legitimate result may be “do not automate this workflow.” That answer must still be worth the diagnostic fee.
