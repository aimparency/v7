# Client Dependence Policy

## Purpose

Paying clients are Aimparency's strongest real-world feedback. They must test
whether the graph-and-model system creates value and survives. They must not
give one client, founder relationship, integration, or provider control over
Aimparency's existence.

## Measures

Review monthly and before accepting a large engagement:

- revenue share of the largest client and largest three clients;
- cash runway with the largest client removed;
- gross margin after model, integration, founder, support, and failure costs;
- accounts-receivable age and payment concentration;
- founder hours and exceptions per client operating loop;
- reusable versus client-specific delivery hours;
- cost and time to deploy the same capability to the next client;
- dependence on one connector, model provider, or client employee;
- percentage of revenue requiring custom work to continue.

## Initial risk thresholds

These are operating hypotheses to revise from evidence:

- Flag when one client exceeds 40% of trailing three-month revenue.
- Block new unfunded expansion when one client exceeds 60%.
- Require deposits or milestone prepayment when client work would consume more
  than one month of runway.
- Do not accept uncapped custom integration or support obligations.
- Do not let outcome-linked fees exceed the fixed revenue needed to cover
  conservative delivery cost.
- Require a documented replacement or exit path for a provider/connector that
  operates a critical loop.

## Response ladder

When concentration rises:

1. Stop treating expansion revenue as guaranteed.
2. Collect outstanding invoices and protect runway.
3. Freeze unpriced bespoke work.
4. Extract the repeated capability into a tested reusable primitive.
5. Sell the diagnostic or primitive to additional qualified owners.
6. Narrow or raise the price of the concentrated client's scope.
7. Prepare an orderly disable/export handoff rather than accepting lock-in.

## Productization test

Client work compounds only if, across comparable deployments:

- deployment time falls;
- founder intervention and exception frequency fall;
- reused capability rises;
- marginal support and model cost fall or remain bounded;
- verified client value and willingness to continue remain positive.

If these do not improve, the graph should prioritize changing the offer,
architecture, target client, or price. Additional clients are not evidence of a
scalable autonomous company when every engagement recreates the work.

## Survival scenarios

Maintain current answers to:

- What stops immediately if the largest client leaves today?
- Which costs can be removed within 7, 30, and 90 days?
- Can every client export its graph, policy, evidence, and runbook?
- Which reusable capabilities remain valuable after a client exits?
- Can a critical loop move to another provider without changing owner intent?
- How many weeks can Aimparency operate with collected cash and contracted
  fixed costs only?

## Graph feedback

Record concentration, margin, reuse, exceptions, and churn as evidence attached
to the relevant business-model and capability aims. Do not automatically rewrite
owner-set intrinsic value from one noisy event. Instead, create or update a
testable hypothesis and let repeated authoritative outcomes change strategy.
