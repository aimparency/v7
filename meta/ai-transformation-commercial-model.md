# AI Transformation Commercial Model

## Positioning

Sell a controlled increase in a company's operating autonomy, not generic AI
consulting and not access to an agent. The client buys an inspectable operating
system that improves a measured business workflow while preserving owner
decision rights.

Prices below are test hypotheses, not validated market facts.

## Staged offer

| Stage | Client buys | Commercial form | Exit artifact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Diagnostic | Mission, constraints, economic baseline, graph, ranked roadmap | Fixed fee | Owner-controlled graph and report |
| Bounded pilot | One reversible operating loop with before/after evidence | Fixed implementation fee plus capped costs | Pilot, logs, evidence, rollback path |
| Rollout | Production operation, monitoring, connectors, training | Milestone fee | Documented operating system |
| Stewardship | Monitoring, graph review, improvements, incident response | Monthly subscription | Ongoing verified operation |
| Outcome component | Share of independently verified incremental value | Optional, capped, time-limited | Evidence statement and calculation |

## Initial price hypotheses

- Diagnostic: EUR 2,500–7,500 depending on company and workflow complexity.
- Pilot: EUR 5,000–20,000 plus client-approved pass-through costs.
- Rollout: scoped after pilot evidence; fixed milestones rather than open-ended
  day rates.
- Stewardship: EUR 1,000–5,000 per month based on operated loops, response
  commitment, and integration burden.
- Outcome component: only where attribution is credible; cap the fee and define
  the baseline, period, exclusions, and authoritative data source in advance.

The first conversations should test willingness to pay and scope, not defend a
price list. Record acceptance, objections, proposed alternatives, and no-decisions
as evidence in the graph.

## Why stage it this way

The diagnostic is valuable without automation and limits sales risk. The pilot
tests technical feasibility and economics before either side commits to a
rollout. Stewardship funds real continuing costs. An outcome component aligns
incentives but must not replace a base fee: Aimparency cannot finance unlimited
client experimentation or accept outcomes it cannot control.

## Economic acceptance rules

Before signing, estimate:

```text
delivery margin = contracted revenue
                - model and software cost
                - integration labour
                - founder and specialist time
                - expected support and exception cost
                - risk reserve
```

Accept an engagement only when:

- the diagnostic or pilot is independently useful to the client;
- expected delivery margin is positive under a conservative cost case;
- the workflow has an authoritative evidence source;
- the maximum loss and responsibility boundary are written;
- client-specific work has a clear price and limit;
- the engagement creates reusable capability or unusually strong learning;
- payment timing does not make Aimparency fund the client's transformation.

## Outcome-linked fee safeguards

Use an outcome component only when all are defined:

- baseline and comparison window;
- metric owner and source system;
- external changes and attribution exclusions;
- treatment of delayed value and reversals;
- maximum fee and payment date;
- client's right to inspect the calculation;
- what happens when evidence is missing or disputed.

Never use agent activity, tool calls, generated tokens, synthetic transactions,
mock revenue, or a self-assigned autonomy score as billable outcomes.

## Ownership and responsibility

The client owns its data, aim graph, exported operating records, and client-
specific configuration. Aimparency retains reusable product code and generalized
patterns without client-confidential content.

The owner remains responsible for values, legal commitments, personnel,
regulated decisions, capital allocation, and any action not explicitly delegated.
Aimparency is responsible for operating within the agreed scope, budgets,
permissions, logs, and escalation rules. Each contract identifies security,
privacy, uptime, incident, and rollback responsibilities explicitly.

## Preventing consultancy drift

Track these per engagement:

- reusable versus client-specific implementation hours;
- founder hours per delivered loop;
- time to deploy the same capability again;
- monthly exceptions requiring manual intervention;
- gross margin after full delivery and support cost;
- renewal and expansion based on verified value;
- revenue concentration by client;
- connector and model-provider concentration.

Pause expansion when founder intervention, custom work, or support cost is not
falling across similar clients. Feed that evidence into graph priority: build a
reusable primitive, narrow the offer, raise the price, or stop selling the case.

## Client qualification

Prefer an owner who has decision authority, a painful recurring workflow,
accessible baseline data, tolerance for a bounded experiment, and willingness
to pay for the diagnostic. Avoid prospects seeking an impressive demo, an
unbounded company-wide transformation, undeclared employee replacement, or
guaranteed outcomes without granting the evidence and authority needed.

## First commercial test

Offer five qualified owners a paid diagnostic. The test succeeds when at least
one signs at a price that covers conservative delivery cost and supplies the
required evidence. Rejections are useful only when reasons are recorded and
change the offer, target profile, price, or roadmap.
