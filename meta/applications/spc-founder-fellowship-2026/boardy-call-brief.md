## Proposed spine to attack

**AI-native teams accumulate direction debt.**

As contexts and agents change, humans repeatedly reconstruct what matters, why
it matters, which trade-offs were already made, and which decisions remain
theirs. Aimparency is repository-owned intent infrastructure: humans express
direction as a durable contribution graph; replaceable agents use it to choose,
verify, and revise work without becoming the source of authority.

The smallest promise is: **change the executor without losing the project's
direction.** The first wedge is a technical founder or small AI-native team
repeatedly using coding agents in one consequential repository. Aimparency wins
only if it reduces founder restatement and wrong-direction rework enough to
outweigh the ceremony of maintaining the graph.

## Evidence / inference boundary

Evidence:

- Running open-source product, repository-owned aim graph, MCP access for
  multiple model families, human gates, phases, prioritization, reflections,
  semantic search, and aim-to-Git evidence.
- The graph has directed real implementation and external applications.
- This application exposed a concrete product defect: human-dependent
  descendants of a phase objective were hidden. The executor fixed it and
  verified the change while the graph preserved human submission authority.

Inference still to test:

- Teams will pay for an independent intent layer.
- The layer becomes more valuable as agents become more capable and replaceable.
- Repeated customer implementations converge into a scalable product.

Do not turn an inference into traction. Do not let the recursive application
example become the headline.
