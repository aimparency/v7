# Three coherent Aimparency narratives

Decision aid for Felix and Boardy. These are mutually exclusive application
spines, not three products to mention together.

## Recommendation

Use **Option A: developer intent infrastructure** as the present-tense company
and **Option B: strategy layer for replaceable agents** as its earned expansion.
Do not lead the SPC application with Option C.

Why: Option A has the strongest direct evidence and the clearest first users.
Option B explains why the product compounds as agent execution becomes abundant.
Option C contains the deepest mission but currently introduces institutional and
security claims far beyond the evidence.

This recommendation changes if Felix can name a real organizational buyer with
an urgent authorized workflow and budget, or if Boardy identifies a sharper wedge
that retains the same invariant.

## Option A — developer intent infrastructure

### One sentence

Aimparency is repository-owned intent infrastructure that lets AI-native software
teams preserve why work matters, what it contributes to, who must decide, and
what evidence changes the plan across long-running and replaceable coding agents.

### First buyer

A technical founder, open-source maintainer, or small AI-native engineering team
running multiple coding-agent sessions against one consequential repository.

### Painful event

The team repeatedly restates project direction; agents complete locally sensible
tasks that no longer serve the mission; rationale disappears into chats; human
decisions surface late; and Git records what changed without recording why.

### Product now

- Repository-owned aim and phase graph.
- Model-neutral MCP surface and multiple executor adapters.
- Value/cost-informed prioritization and transitive phase context.
- Explicit human-dependent states.
- Reflections and commit-linked implementation evidence.

### Proof

The product directs its own repository work, survives executor/session changes,
and exposed and corrected a human-gate visibility defect during this application.

### Eight-week test

Recruit ten qualified AI-native repositories; observe one live coordination
failure in each; install in at least three; measure founder restatements, wrong-
direction work, delayed human gates, and continued usage; obtain at least one paid
commitment or a clear budget rejection.

### Expansion

Once a team trusts the graph as the authoritative account of direction, more
specialist agents and adjacent repositories can operate through it. The product
becomes the strategy layer in a network of executors.

### Fatal weakness

Users may experience this as extra project-management ceremony. Agent vendors or
existing developer tools may absorb enough memory and planning to remove the pain.
Only observed repeated use and willingness to pay answer this.

### What must not be said

- “We already coordinate autonomous organizations.”
- “The graph guarantees alignment.”
- “Git linkage proves an aim was achieved.”

## Option B — strategy layer for replaceable agents

### One sentence

Aimparency is the model-independent strategy layer that preserves authoritative
human objectives while replaceable specialist agents plan, act, return evidence,
and hand work to one another.

### First buyer

A technical organization already operating multiple agent runtimes or building
an internal agent platform, where prompts and vendor-specific memory fragment the
organization's objectives.

### Painful event

Each executor receives a local prompt but no durable account of how its work
serves the institution; handoffs lose rationale; evidence does not revise shared
priorities; changing vendors means changing institutional memory.

### Product now

The existing graph and MCP tools implement the local strategy side: objectives,
contribution relationships, priority context, phases, human gates, reflection,
and evidence. Multiple wrapped-agent adapters demonstrate executor independence.

### Proof

A replacement executor can recover the SPC objective, mission paths, deadline,
verified evidence, and human authority boundaries without relying on the previous
chat. This is a continuity proof inside one project—not yet multi-organization
interoperability.

### Eight-week test

Run controlled handoffs across at least two executors on consequential repository
work; compare missing-context questions, restatements, reversals, and verified
outcome quality against a prompt/task baseline; partner with one real multi-agent
team willing to expose its orchestration failure.

### Expansion

The strategy graph becomes a stable control plane above changing models, agent
frameworks, and specialist tools. External agent protocols may carry bounded
delegations and evidence while Aimparency retains institutional direction.

### Fatal weakness

The target customer may be too early or too small. “Strategy agent” may sound
like another orchestration framework, while the current proof occurs inside one
repository controlled by one founder. Cross-executor adapter code is not evidence
of cross-organization demand.

### What must not be said

- “A2A coordination works in production.”
- “All supported model runtimes are equally reliable.”
- “Aimparency is the operating system for agents.”

## Option C — constitutional layer for autonomous organizations

### One sentence

Aimparency is an inspectable constitutional layer through which owners and
participants express purpose, authority, obligations, and evidence while AI
systems increasingly operate the organization.

### First buyer

An owner-led organization delegating a bounded, economically meaningful workflow
to AI while needing human decisions, relationship constraints, auditability, and
rollback to remain explicit.

### Painful event

Automation expands from individual tasks into operational decisions, but the
organization has no shared machine-readable account of who may decide what, which
human relationships cannot be optimized away, or how observed outcomes should
change strategy.

### Product now

Aimparency already represents aims, contribution structure, human-dependent
states, phases, evidence, and reflection. Internal operating-model documents
specify owner constitution, bounded loops, authoritative systems, rollback, and
economic telemetry.

### Proof

The SPC run shows a weak form of constitutional behavior: the executor can change
code and plans but cannot supply founder truth, accept investment terms, or submit
institutional representations. This is suggestive—not an organizational
deployment or enforceable constitution.

### Eight-week test

Secure one owner-authorized bounded workflow; write explicit decision rights,
credentials, stop conditions, baseline, economics, and rollback; let the system
perform real work; measure owner intervention, errors, value, and trust; obtain a
paid continuation or a clear rejection.

### Expansion

Repeated workflows reveal reusable governance primitives. Aimparency could become
the intent and accountability substrate for increasingly autonomous companies
without making the executing AI the source of legitimacy.

### Fatal weakness

The narrative outruns both product enforcement and market evidence. “Constitution”
invites legal, security, organizational-design, and legitimacy questions the
current planning graph cannot answer. The first workflow risks looking like
consulting unrelated to the software wedge.

### What must not be said

- “Human sovereignty is guaranteed.”
- “Aimparency safely runs companies.”
- “Wilde Möhre or Herzog Records is a customer.”

## Comparison

| Criterion | A · Developer intent | B · Agent strategy | C · Organizational constitution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present product evidence | strongest | credible local subset | suggestive primitives only |
| First-user specificity | strongest | narrower, emerging buyer set | workflow-specific but diffuse |
| Immediate installability | strongest | moderate | low without service work |
| Venture-scale expansion | credible through agent growth | strongest direct vision | largest but most speculative |
| Competitive explanation | developer tools + agent memory | orchestration/control plane | consulting/governance/automation |
| Main risk | feature/ceremony | market too early | vision outruns evidence |
| Recommended application role | headline | expansion | long-term implication at most |

## Decision test

Choose the option for which Felix can complete all four statements with specific
evidence:

1. “I have repeatedly seen **[painful event]**.”
2. “I built **[current product]**, and this artifact proves it.”
3. “Next week I can recruit these ten **[first users]**.”
4. “If they repeatedly use/pay for it, the product compounds toward **[expansion]**.”

If Option A wins, keep the deepest mission as founder motivation, not application
surface area. If Option B or C wins, Boardy should demand evidence strong enough
to justify giving up Option A's clarity.
