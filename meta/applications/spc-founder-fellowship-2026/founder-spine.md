# Founder spine: from writing code to directing abundant execution

Status: candidate interpretation requiring Felix's confirmation. Repository and
prior first-person evidence are separated below.

## The causal thesis

The strongest founder story is not “Felix has always liked goals” or “Felix is a
good programmer.” It is a transition in the nature of his own work:

> I used to write loads of code. These days, AI writes the code.

That sentence appears in Felix's earlier first-person application material. If
it still feels true, it gives Aimparency a causal origin. Felix has experienced
execution becoming abundant inside his own craft. The scarce work moved upward:
choosing what deserves to exist, preserving decisions across sessions, judging
whether locally good output serves the larger purpose, and accepting
responsibility for external claims.

Aimparency is then not a speculative response to future agents. It is the system
Felix built because his daily role changed before the surrounding project tools
did.

## The distinctive personal tension

The earlier material also describes a life of improvisation alongside years of
developing a personal decision practice. Subject to confirmation, that tension is
more distinctive than a conventional technical-founder biography:

- improvisation creates optionality, exploration, and unusual combinations;
- a decision system protects direction when many possibilities compete;
- AI multiplies possible action faster than a human can supervise it;
- Aimparency attempts to preserve the freedom of exploration without losing the
  explicit structure needed for consequential execution.

technical construction, aesthetic judgment, adaptation, and performance under

## Three founder claims, ranked

### 1. Lived transition — recommended spine

Felix experienced his work changing from producing code to directing AI-produced
work, recognized that direction and authority lacked durable infrastructure, and
built Aimparency to solve that problem in his own repository.

Evidence: prior first-person statement; working product; repository history;
model co-author trailers; live graph. Human confirmation: whether this was the
actual causal sequence rather than a compelling retrospective interpretation.

### 2. Improviser with a decision discipline — memorable support

Felix has combined broad experimentation with a long-running practice for
choosing among possible lives and projects. Aimparency externalizes that practice
for an era when AI can execute more possibilities than one person can evaluate.

Evidence: earlier first-person application only. Human confirmation: duration,
practice, one concrete decision, and what actually transferred into the product.

### 3. AI-native principal builder — execution proof

Felix directed and integrated a substantial working system across frontend,
backend, graph algorithms, MCP, several executor adapters, retrieval, and Git
evidence while explicitly using models as collaborators.

Evidence: 699 locally reachable commits authored under Felix's Git identity, 135
with Claude co-author trailers, plus inspectable implementation. Limitation: Git
metadata is not sole intellectual authorship or product quality.
