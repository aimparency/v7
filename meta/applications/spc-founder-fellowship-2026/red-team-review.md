# Red-team review: why SPC might reject Aimparency

This memo argues against the application. Its purpose is not pessimism; it is to
prevent elegant language from hiding unresolved company questions.

## Current verdict

**Interesting founder/product experiment; not yet a clearly inevitable Founder
Fellowship company.**

The application can become compelling if it presents one sharp present-tense
product, shows why Felix has earned the insight, and treats the fellowship as a
specific company-building experiment. It becomes weak if it asks the reviewer to
fund a philosophy, admire recursion, or infer demand from technical depth.

## Rejection reason 1: “This is a personal ontology disguised as infrastructure.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- “Aim,” “intent layer,” “direction,” and “human authority” are abstract.
- Felix's long-running decision-system practice makes the origin authentic but
  can also suggest a private worldview searching for a market.
- The product contains many primitives before one external workflow is proven.

Minimum credible response:

- Name one recurring failure in an AI-native technical team.
- Show the moment existing prompts, issues, or agent memory failed.
- Demonstrate why a graph changed the outcome, not merely its description.

Do not respond with a larger vision. Respond with a concrete before/after.

## Rejection reason 2: “A better agent memory or project-management feature will absorb this.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Coding agents increasingly preserve plans, rules, tasks, and summaries.
- GitHub, Linear, Notion, and agent vendors already own adjacent surfaces.
- A standalone graph creates adoption and synchronization costs.

Minimum credible response:

- Explain which invariant must remain independent of the executing model.
- Show how one objective survives switching executors or losing chat history.
- State what Aimparency owns that a task list does not: authority, contribution
  structure, priority inheritance, and evidence-aware revision.
- Admit that the initial interface may integrate with existing tools rather than
  replace them.

The moat cannot be “we use a graph.” It must emerge from accumulated, trusted
intent/evidence state and the behavior it enables.

## Rejection reason 3: “The recursive application proves very little.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Any capable coding agent can create files, run tests, and summarize context.
- The executor and graph live in one repository under one founder's supervision.
- There is no controlled comparison showing less drift or better outcomes.

Minimum credible response:

- Keep recursion to one compact example.
- Point to the non-obvious correction: a real human-gate visibility defect was
  discovered and fixed under the application objective.
- Make the falsifiable continuity claim narrow: a replacement executor can
  recover purpose, evidence, and authority boundaries.
- Explicitly deny that this proves demand, economic value, or general autonomous
  coordination.

If the application needs recursion to feel novel, the company story is not ready.

## Rejection reason 4: “There is no customer.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Wilde Möhre and Herzog Records are hypotheses and relationships, not users.
- Robin's Leipzig automation work is a possible channel, not Aimparency demand.
- The repository demonstrates founder usage, not third-party retention or
  willingness to pay.

Minimum credible response:

- Do not manufacture traction language.
- Name the first buyer hypothesis precisely and the ten people Felix can contact.
- Define the paid result and a short disconfirmation test.
- Explain why SPC materially accelerates that test.

Honest absence of traction can be acceptable early. Vagueness about how to seek
traction is harder to forgive.

## Rejection reason 5: “The wedge and the global maximum are different companies.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- The materials move among developer tooling, organizational workflows,
  multi-agent strategy, owner-governed companies, and ASI legitimacy.
- A festival closeout diagnostic does not obviously lead to developer
  infrastructure.
- “Constitutional layer for autonomous organizations” can sound inflated before
  one organization uses it.

Minimum credible response:

- Choose one product invariant across wedge and expansion: durable, inspectable
  human intent governing replaceable execution.
- Pick one initial environment where the problem is already acute.
- Describe later markets as consequences if the invariant proves valuable, not
  parallel go-to-market plans.

The global maximum should clarify the direction of compounding, not enlarge the
current claim.

## Rejection reason 6: “Founder commitment and team status are ambiguous.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Prior materials alternate between Felix alone and Felix/Robin at 50/50.
- Robin's current commitment is not confirmed.
- Full-time and relocation readiness are undecided.
- The product's breadth can resemble an intellectually rich side project.

Minimum credible response:

- State founder configuration with no ambiguous “we.”
- State what Felix is stopping or choosing in order to pursue this.
- Confirm the in-person/full-time commitment if true.
- Use one or two difficult shipped artifacts to show execution intensity.

This cannot be repaired with prose. It requires a human commitment.

## Rejection reason 7: “The governance language outruns the actual safety model.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- `human-dependent` is a useful orchestration state, not a security boundary.
- An agent with broad tool permissions may still act outside the planning graph.
- “Human sovereignty” can sound categorical and unearned.

Minimum credible response:

- Say “inspectable orchestration and authority boundary,” not guaranteed control.
- Separate aim state from underlying tool permissions and institutional authority.
- Show where the executor stopped because submission and personal truth belonged
  to Felix.
- Present stronger authorization enforcement as a product direction if it is not
  yet implemented.

Precision here increases credibility; it does not weaken the vision.

## Rejection reason 8: “The founder story explains interests, not unfair advantage.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Coding since 11 and AI-native development are common founder claims.
- Festival visuals and an improvisational life are memorable but not yet tied to
  company-building ability.
- The application currently describes more product architecture than founder
  learning.

Minimum credible response:

- Connect the practiced decision system to one specific insight competitors miss.
- Show an unusual artifact with inspectable quality.
- Name a decision Felix changed because reality contradicted the system.
- Explain why Felix will keep pursuing this problem through an uncomfortable
  market test, not only continue building.

The strongest founder story is a causal chain, not a list of experiences.

## Rejection reason 9: “The SPC learning agenda is generic.”

Why a reviewer may think it:

- Funding, network, feedback, and customers are benefits of every accelerator.
- The current material does not yet say what must be true after eight weeks.

Minimum credible response:

Choose two or three falsifiable outcomes, such as:

1. Ten qualified AI-native technical teams expose one real coordination failure;
   at least three install or permit observation of Aimparency in a live repo.
2. At least one team pays for the intent layer or a tightly bounded implementation;
   otherwise document why the problem does not own budget.
3. A second executor completes a consequential run from graph state with
   measurably fewer founder restatements than a chat/task baseline.

Felix and Boardy must choose the actual outcomes. These are rigorous defaults,
not facts or commitments.

## What to delete first

If space is tight, delete in this order:

1. General claims about ASI or autonomous organizations.
2. Repeated explanation of the recursive application.
3. Long feature inventories.
4. Unconfirmed named relationships.
5. Market-size extrapolations from coding assistants.

Protect:

1. The founder insight.
2. One concrete current product claim.
3. One hard technical/operational proof.
4. One specific first user and experiment.
5. The causal founder story.
6. The exact reason SPC changes the trajectory now.

## Go/no-go test before submission

The application is ready for final human review only if a skeptical reader can
answer all six in one sentence each:

- What exists now?
- Who urgently needs it first?
- Why is the existing stack insufficient?
- Why is Felix unusually equipped and committed?
- What evidence is real, and what remains a hypothesis?
- What exactly will the fellowship make knowable?

If any answer requires “it is complicated,” revise the narrative spine before
polishing fields.
