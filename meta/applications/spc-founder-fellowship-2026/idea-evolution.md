# How Aimparency generates and discards ideas

Purpose: evidence for SPC's interest in how an applicant explores. This records
project decisions visible in the graph. It does not prove Felix's personal
motivation or agreement; he must confirm which decisions reflect his thinking.

## The operating method

Aimparency keeps rejected ideas in the graph rather than deleting them. A
cancelled aim records why a direction was rejected and what evidence would
justify reviving it. Semantic review can then show that history when a future
executor proposes similar work. The intended loop is:

1. connect an idea to the larger purpose it claims to serve;
2. state a concrete user, failure, or outcome;
3. compare the new machinery with primitives already present;
4. seek external evidence proportional to the implementation cost;
5. implement and verify, narrow, or cancel with a revival condition;
6. preserve the rejection so context loss does not resurrect it as novelty.

This is more interesting than listing unrelated ideas because it makes changing
one's mind part of the product's operational memory.

## Documented rejections

### Autonomous will → human-directed execution

Cancelled aim: `133934ba-f325-4b92-b57c-517bb1ea497f`.

The graph once contained “Develop Aligned Autonomous Will.” It was cancelled as
vague and conceptually misleading. The product now treats humans and
organizations as the source of intent; agents may propose supporting aims, but
do not acquire a separate will merely because they execute autonomously.

This is the deepest correction: autonomy became a capability in service of human
direction, not the product's source of legitimacy.

### Agent swarm roles → ordinary graph coordination

Cancelled aim: `0e646aa6-4ff7-48b8-a5d2-178cfb96c6a1`.

A planner/developer/QA/deployment swarm sounded demonstrative, especially for a
hackathon, but lacked a validated workflow. The role taxonomy was rejected as
architecture noise. Ordinary aims, contribution edges, statuses, reflections,
and human gates remain the coordination primitives until a real failure proves
they are insufficient.

### First-class plan structures → minimal contribution graph

Cancelled aim: `5d8044b4-fc4e-440f-bdb3-fbc3b0e00f03`.

Explicit multi-step plan objects would have duplicated the existing graph.
Dependencies, alternatives, fallbacks, and ordering can be expressed as aims and
explained contribution edges. The idea was rejected to protect conceptual
minimalism rather than add a feature merely because it sounded agentic.

### Generic information ingestion → research for a concrete aim

Cancelled aim: `3045f505-1214-4618-82f3-d36544327059`.

A persistent RSS/web information-stream subsystem had no named recurring source
or decision it improved. It was cancelled in favor of research on demand for a
specific mission. Revival requires a real monitoring workflow with measurable
value.

### On-chain agent identity → demand-gated interoperability

Cancelled aim: `ca0cd74f-fe9c-44d1-9fa9-85fca97439de`.

ERC-8004 identity work survived from an expired hackathon direction but had no
current user, interoperability partner, or economic requirement. It was
cancelled rather than letting ecosystem novelty determine the roadmap. Revival
requires an external partner or workflow that actually needs it.

## Current strategic narrowing

The application materials considered three present-tense narratives:

1. developer intent infrastructure;
2. model-independent strategy layer for replaceable agents;
3. constitutional layer for autonomous organizations.

The third was not declared false. It was removed from the present-tense claim
because product enforcement and market evidence do not support it. The second is
an earned expansion. The first is the current wedge because it has a working
product, reachable users, and a falsifiable handoff failure.

This is a useful distinction: discard unsupported implementation; demote an
unproven vision; preserve both as context rather than oscillating between hype
and amnesia.
